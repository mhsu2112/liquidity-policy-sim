Limits

Disclosure. This project's sponsor, Mike Hsu, publicly advocated a version of Option B (a standalone discount window readiness requirement) while serving as Acting Comptroller of the Currency. The comparison rules and hypotheses are published before any results are produced, Option C's specification is reviewed by an outside supporter of LCR recognition, and results are reported whether or not they match the hypotheses.

The v1 limits recorded so far.

  1. Runs slow down after the first morning. Under the confidence rule, a run starts hard and then fades. The model can't produce a run that accelerates the way SVB's did on day 2 (tuning fit −37% on cumulative outflows), and the First Republic-like run is too mild. (Clarification 8; tuning report (M1.10); validation report (M1.11))
  2. Some behavioral settings are not identified. The SVB pattern can't pin down the wholesale roll threshold or the fire-sale price impact, so both keep their pre-tuning values. Results that turn on wholesale refusals or securities sales rest on those starting values. (Clarification 15; config/params_frozen.yaml)
  3. Disclosures move no one. Scheduled ratio disclosures (B's five-day ratio, the LCR including C's credit) have no effect on depositors or lenders in v1. C's reported LCR neither reassures nor misleads observers, and keeping B's ratio private has no effect by construction. (Clarification 9; Clarification 14 item 4)
  4. B′ is exploratory. B′ (B's ratio counting loans at the Fed only) was added after pre-registration. No hypothesis covers it, and every B′ result is exploratory. (Amendment 3)
  5. Cliff-edge dynamics may produce ties. A bank either stays above the confidence tolerance and nobody leaves, or falls below it and runs. Banks well clear of the edge behave the same under every policy, so many paired comparisons may tie because nothing a policy changes is reached, not because the policies are equivalent. (docs/notes/svb-false-alarm-note.md; validation report (M1.11))
  6. No weekends. Every half-day is a business day, so no bank gets a weekend to mobilize collateral. No BTFP and no contagion between banks either. (Clarification 17; Amendment 6)
  7. The grace count is an upper bound. The count of timing-only failures (cash agreed and arriving by the next morning would have covered the gap, equity ≥ 0) is an upper bound, because the bank's own decisions assume the strict failure rule. It is never used to decide which policy leads. (Amendment 6)
  8. The 2007–09 corpus is short. The calibration corpus holds 110 passages from 2007–09, below the 150 minimum, because the public sources are exhausted. That period is thinner than the others in the corpus and the gold set. (Clarification 24 item 4 (m2-signals branch))
  9. The Jev reference is provisional (v0.1). The 0.78 market-stigma reference comes from Jev readings checked only against another AI model's labels (model-to-model agreement), not against human readers. It is shown as a reference point; the full stigma sweep is the result. (Amendment 7; Clarification 30; Clarification 31)
  10. Official support peak equals total. Window loans are not repaid within a 30-day episode, so the peak window balance equals the total lent. Both columns are shown; they carry the same information. (Clarification 10 item 8; Clarification 31)
  11. The results depend on the strict failure rule. A bank fails the moment it cannot pay on time, even if cash it has already arranged arrives the next morning. Counting those timing-only failures as survivals (an upper bound), B's advantage over A disappears, and for SVB-like banks it reverses. Part of that reversal is an artefact: a run that fails for timing alone stops there, so the later outflows it would have faced are never seen. (Amendment 6; Amendment 9 (session v0.1-D diagnostics))
  12. Model banks never hesitate to borrow. In every policy, bank type and scenario, no run borrowed a day or more after its own projection first showed a shortfall. The channel by which stigma makes banks wait too long is therefore not tested. Stigma acts here only through how the market reads a draw once it is known. (Amendment 9 (session v0.1-D diagnostics))
  13. C's gains for banks without LCR credit rest on the routine-borrowing assumption. Banks without LCR credit (the diversified regionals) gain under C only because C's routine-borrowing rate lowers everyone's effective stigma. With no routine-borrowing effect (s = 0) those gains vanish, and Jev's reading of C's disclosure texts implies s ≈ 0 for C (0.003; provisional, exploratory). (Clarification 31; Amendment 8; Amendment 9 (session v0.1-D diagnostics))
